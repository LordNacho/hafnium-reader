Round three: Copenhagen landmarks

Android’s round crop clips the tips of some feed waves. The final icon gets its own Android layout, so ignore that when choosing.

Material to draw on

Element 72, Hf
A silvery, lustrous metal. Its periodic-table tile (72 · Hf · 178.49) is a ready-made badge.
Named after Copenhagen
Hafnia is the Latin name for Copenhagen, and havn means harbour: København is the “merchants’ harbour”. Things arrive there and are kept safe.
Found in Copenhagen, 1923
Dirk Coster and George de Hevesy discovered it at Niels Bohr’s institute, guided by Bohr’s atomic theory. The arcs of a Bohr atom echo the RSS feed waves.
Electron shells 2 · 8 · 18 · 32 · 10 · 2
Six shells around the nucleus. Three of them, cut to a quarter, are the feed symbol.
Copenhagen’s colours
Nyhavn’s ochre, red and blue houses on the water; copper spires gone verdigris green; the corkscrew spire of the Church of Our Saviour.
Hidden in plain sight
Hafnium oxide insulates the transistors in modern phone chips, so it is already inside the phone the app runs on. It hides in zirconium ore and was missed for decades.
Church of Our Saviour
A black and gold spire with a staircase winding round the outside up to a gilded globe. The green in draft J comes from the city’s copper roofs instead.
Round Tower
Rundetårn, from the 1640s: a brick observatory climbed by a spiral ramp instead of stairs, and Europe’s oldest working observatory.
Copper roofs
Verdigris domes and spires across the skyline: the Marble Church dome, Rosenborg, City Hall, and the Stock Exchange, whose spire burned in 2024 and is being rebuilt.
Nyhavn and the harbour
Flat-fronted pastel houses with pitched tile roofs, and tall-masted wooden ships on the canal. The Black Diamond library sits on the harbour front.
Off limits
The Little Mermaid statue is still under copyright held by the sculptor’s heirs, Tivoli is a trademark, and the city’s coat of arms is protected, so these are only borrowed as ideas.
