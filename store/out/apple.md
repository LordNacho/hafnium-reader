## Description

Quietfeed is a simple RSS reader for the sites, blogs and news sources you choose. There are no ads, no account to create and nothing to sign in to. Articles are saved on your phone, so you can keep reading on a plane, on the subway, or anywhere your connection drops.

Already using another reader? Export your subscriptions as an OPML file from The Old Reader, Feedly, Inoreader or most other services, and import it into Quietfeed. Your folders come along too. You can export your list again at any time from the share sheet.

Adding a single feed is easy: paste a feed address, or just the site's homepage, and Quietfeed will look for its feed. It reads RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0 (RDF) and Atom.

Reading
• A clean article view with previous and next, star, mark read or unread, and share
• Open the original page in the in-app browser
• System, light, dark or sepia theme
• Text size from 85% to 150% on top of your phone's setting, sans or serif font, compact, normal or relaxed line spacing, images on or off
• Unread or all articles, mark all as read, folders, All articles and Starred

Staying up to date
• Refreshes when you open the app, and in the background when your phone allows it
• Choose a minimum time between background refreshes, from 15 minutes to 12 hours, and limit them to Wi-Fi if you like
• Asks each site whether its feed has changed before downloading it again, to save data

Keeping storage in check
• Keep the newest 25 to 500 articles per feed, and articles from the last 7 to 365 days
• Starred articles are never removed
• Delete read articles in one tap

Article text is stored for offline reading. Images load from the web when you're online.

Your privacy
Quietfeed doesn't collect anything. There is no analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking. The app connects only to the feeds and websites you add, directly from your device, and to pages you choose to open. Nothing is sent to the developer. Everything stays on your phone, and deleting the app deletes it.

Quietfeed is free, with no in-app purchases.

## Age rating notes

The app has no user-generated content of its own, no chat, no purchases and no ads. It shows articles from feeds the user adds, and it can open the original article page in an in-app browser. Answer the age rating questionnaire honestly about that browser: Apple treats "Unrestricted Web Access" as a separate question, and answering Yes raises the rating. The app is not directed at children.

## App Privacy

Data Not Collected. Everything is stored on the device, and the app has no analytics, crash reporting, accounts or third-party SDKs; it only contacts the feed and web addresses the user adds or opens.

## Review Notes

No login or account is needed. A fresh install has no feeds, so please add one to try the app:

1. Tap Add and paste a feed address, for example https://www.theverge.com/rss/index.xml or https://xkcd.com/atom.xml (a site's homepage also works; the app looks for its feed).
2. Alternatively, import an OPML subscription file from the Files app.

Articles are stored on the device and can be read offline once downloaded. Background refresh is scheduled by iOS and needs a physical device; it does not run reliably in the Simulator.
